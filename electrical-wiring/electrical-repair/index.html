Repair, partial rewire or full rewire

Old wiring is not, by itself, a reason to rewire a whole building.

The deciding question is how much original wire is still live and what condition it is in, not the year the building went up.

  • Fix the failed point

    A burnt device, a loose splice or one damaged section is replaced, and that circuit is tested end to end.

  • Rewire the worst circuits

    Kitchen, bath and laundry circuits carry the heaviest loads, so they are usually the first candidates for new wire.

  • Rewire the building

    When most circuits are original and brittle, or walls are coming open for a remodel anyway.

Site assessment

What gets checked on a repair call

A symptom shows where to start looking, not always where the fault is. These are the places a repair usually ends up.

  1. 01

    Heat at the device

    A warm cover plate, a darkened outlet or a melted plug points to a loose or failing connection at that spot.

  2. 02

    Push-in terminals upstream

    Push-in connections on older devices can loosen with years of use. A dead outlet can trace back to the one before it on the circuit.

  3. 03

    Splices in boxes and ceilings

    Twisted or taped joins, and joins left inside a wall with no box, fail often. NEC 300.15 requires a box at each splice point.

  4. 04

    Shared neutrals on multiwire circuits

    Older wiring sometimes runs two circuits on one shared neutral. If that neutral opens, one circuit can get too much voltage and the other too little.

  5. 05

    Breaker and panel terminals

    A breaker that runs hot, buzzes or sits loose on the bus, or a scorched lug, is dealt with before anything downstream.

  6. 06

    Condition of the insulation

    Rubber and cloth insulation on original wire hardens near fixtures and boxes, where heat has baked it for decades.

  7. 07

    Earlier amateur work

    A larger breaker on a small wire, mixed wire sizes or an extension cord run through a wall can explain repeat failures.

How urgent is it?

  1. 1

    Call 911 and get out

    Smoke, flames, or sparking that does not stop. Nobody should open the panel or touch the device.

  3. 2

    Breaker off, call an electrician now

    A burning smell, a hot or discolored outlet or switch, a buzzing breaker, or lights that flare bright. Switch off that circuit only if the panel is dry and cool to reach.

  5. 3

    Book it this week

    A dead outlet or circuit with no heat or smell, a GFCI that will not reset, or a breaker that trips now and then.

  7. 4

    Plan it

    Original wiring that works and shows no damage, found in a home inspection or ahead of a remodel.

When in doubt, treat it as urgent. A burning smell with no visible source still needs a call the same day.

Repair the device, or more?

Heat damage is cut out, never cleaned up.

  • A scorched device is replaced, and the wire behind it is cut back past any darkened insulation.

  • If the insulation is still brittle further along, that run gets new wire.

  • A device on aluminum wire needs one marked CO/ALR, or a connector listed for aluminum.

Ice, snow or a branch pulled at the service

A weatherhead or mast pulled loose by a storm can cut power to part of a building or leave lights flickering. ComEd's drop wires end at the weatherhead; the mast, the weatherhead and the meter socket are usually the building owner's to repair before ComEd reconnects. Downed or sagging wires are ComEd's to handle, and nobody should go near them.

01Does a Chicago home with old wiring need a full rewire?

Only if the circuits fail the checks that matter. At opened boxes those are insulation that cracks when moved, heat marks on devices and wire ends, and terminals that have worked loose. A rewire quote should name the circuits that failed and say why. Highlights Chicago can test the circuits in question and price new wire only for the ones that need it.

02Can a two-prong outlet be swapped for a three-prong one?

Only in certain ways. A three-prong outlet on a circuit with no ground wire is a hazard. The NEC allows a GFCI outlet marked "No Equipment Ground" in its place, which protects people but grounds nothing. Running a ground wire, or confirming a sound conduit ground, is the other route. Highlights Chicago can install either fix.

03Is it normal for a dimmer switch to feel warm?

Slightly warm, yes. Dimmers shed some heat through the faceplate, more so with several lamps on one control. Hot to the touch, buzzing, flickering or a discolored plate is not normal and points to an overloaded dimmer or a loose connection. That dimmer should be switched off at the breaker until it is checked.

04Who is allowed to do permitted electrical repairs in Chicago?

A licensed electrical contractor (general) is required for most electrical work, per the City's Express Permit Program instructions. The City's terms add that the work must be done by the Chicago-licensed contractor listed on the permit. Highlights Chicago is a licensed and insured electrical contractor.

05Can an antique light fixture stay when old wiring is replaced?

Often, yes. Old fixtures usually carry their own cloth or rubber leads, and those are the part heat damages. New fixture wire, a sound socket and a box rated for the fixture's weight let it go back up. LED lamps run cooler than the bulbs the fixture was built for.

06What should a home buyer do when an inspection flags old wiring?

Get the flagged circuits identified and priced before the deadline to negotiate. A home inspector notes what is visible; an electrician can open boxes and test the circuits behind the report. Knowing which circuits need repair and which need new wire puts a real figure on the problem before closing.

07Why do lights dim for a moment when the air conditioner starts?

A brief dip when a large motor starts is common on an older service, because the motor draws a surge for a split second. Lasting dimming, dimming across several rooms, or flicker at other times is different. It points to a loose connection or an undersized service, which needs an electrician to trace.

08Do you cover my part of Chicago?

We work citywide, with the largest share of our permit record in Wards 1, 32, 26, 47 and 27 on the north and northwest sides. We also hold current registration in Evanston and Palatine.

Why the age matters electrically

A building from 1906 was wired, if it was wired at all, for lighting. Everything since — the refrigerator, the window units, the home office, the induction range, the car — has been added to a service that was never designed for it. That is the single thread running through most Chicago electrical work.

Township by township

West Chicago is the oldest at 83% pre-1940 across 65,664 properties. Lake View follows at 71% across 23,046. Jefferson township — Portage Park, Jefferson Park, Irving Park — is younger at 60% pre-1940 but far larger at 112,923 properties, and its median year of 1927 puts most of it in the bungalow era. Rogers Park is the youngest of the north-side townships at a 1938 median.

What that means room by room

188,389 properties citywide are two-storey and 154,926 single-storey; 324,305 have full basements, which is where the panel usually lives and where access is usually straightforward. 107,336 properties have no garage at all — relevant the moment an EV charger enters the conversation. And 335,769 use a warm-air furnace against 98,849 on hot water or steam, which decides what kind of equipment circuits exist already.

Old wiring, conduit and the rules behind repairs

Old wiring raises questions that one repair visit rarely settles: what can be reused, what can be added, and what has to change first.

Why can many Chicago rewires reuse the old conduit?

Often because the wire was never run open. Chicago's electrical code has long called for metal raceway in most buildings, so original wire in older Chicago buildings often sits inside steel pipe. Where that pipe is continuous and undamaged, new wire can often be pulled through it and the plaster stays put. For Highlights Chicago, the state of the conduit at the first opened box shows how invasive a rewire will be.

Chicago adopts the 2017 National Electrical Code through Title 14E of its Municipal Code, with local amendments. Its rules have long favored metal conduit over the plastic-sheathed cable common in the suburbs. In Lake View township, which takes in the Lake View community area and its neighbors, the Cook County Assessor dates 70.9% of houses and two-to-six-unit buildings to before 1940.

The usual rewire quote assumes the walls come open. Every run is fished through plaster and lath, every opening is patched, and the job grows to fit the patching.

Steel conduit changes the math. If the pipe is intact with tight joints and gentle bends, the old conductors can be drawn out. New ones are then pulled in from box to box. Crushed, rusted or broken runs still have to be replaced.

Reusing the conduit does not make the job informal. Highlights Chicago is the contractor of record on the City permit. New wire pulled through old pipe is filed and then checked by a City electrical inspector, like any other rewire.

Can new circuits be added to old wiring in Chicago?

Only after defects in what they tie into are fixed. The City's terms for Express Permit electrical work say deficiencies in the existing system, including grounding and bonding, must be corrected as part of the permitted work. So a new circuit or fixture in an old building can start with a repair. Highlights Chicago works under those terms on its City permits.

Kitchens gain appliances, basements become living space and window units give way to central air. Each change asks old circuits and an old service to carry more than they were built for.

The usual shortcut is to tap the nearest outlet or junction box and extend from there. It is quick, and it hides a brittle splice or an overloaded circuit behind fresh drywall.

Chicago puts that risk on the contractor. The same terms, effective September 16, 2024, make the contractor on the permit responsible for investigating existing conditions first. That contractor must also make sure the circuit or panel can carry the added load (chicago.gov, read October 17, 2026).

Highlights Chicago holds City of Chicago electrical contractor licenses ECC96456 and ECC94521, both active on the City register. A permit application names its contractor by exact name or Chicago license number.

Can insulation be blown into walls that still have knob-and-tube wiring?

Not around live knob-and-tube conductors. The 2017 NEC, which Chicago adopts, bars knob-and-tube wiring in hollow walls, ceilings and attics where loose, rolled or foamed insulation surrounds the wires. So an insulation project in an older home starts by finding out whether any original wiring is still live. Highlights Chicago can test those circuits and replace the live runs before the insulation crew arrives.

Knob-and-tube was designed to shed heat into open air. Its two wires run apart on porcelain supports, with nothing around them but the empty cavity.

Energy upgrades often fill attics and wall cavities with blown-in insulation. The insulation crew sees the attic floor, not the inside of every wall, and may not know which circuits are original.

Packed insulation traps heat against old rubber and cloth. NEC 394.12 lists insulated hollow spaces among the places knob-and-tube may not be used. That makes the order of work a code question, not a preference.

Replacing those runs is permitted electrical work. Customers describe Highlights Chicago jobs passing City inspection, the check that confirms new wiring before insulation covers it.
