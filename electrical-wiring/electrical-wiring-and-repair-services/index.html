Four common routes for new wire

All at once, by floor, or with a remodel

How a rewire is phased matters as much as how much wire it replaces.

Phasing keeps a lived-in home usable. The order usually follows which circuits carry the heaviest loads and which walls open first.

  • One floor at a time

    Each floor gets new circuits and goes back into service before the next floor starts, so the household keeps working rooms.

  • The whole house in one stretch

    Every original circuit comes out together, often with a panel change. It moves fastest when the home can stand empty for the work.

  • Alongside a remodel

    The rewire follows the remodel schedule in the rooms being redone, and the rooms the remodel leaves alone can be phased later.

Site assessment

What gets checked before a rewire is priced

The year a building went up says little. What sets the scope is how much original wire is still live, and where new wire can travel.

  1. 01

    Which circuits are still original

    Whether knob-and-tube or cloth-covered wire still feeds a circuit, and which rooms that circuit serves.

  2. 02

    Whether old conduit can be re-pulled

    Where original wire runs in steel pipe, the state of that pipe at the first opened boxes shows whether new wire can follow it.

  3. 03

    Joins between old and newer wire

    Earlier remodels often tied new cable onto original runs inside a wall. Those joins decide where a partial rewire can safely stop.

  4. 04

    Routes between floors

    Closets stacked above each other, the basement ceiling and the attic decide how wire reaches each floor without opening finished rooms.

  5. 05

    Wall condition

    Sound walls take fished cable through small cuts. Loose or sagging finishes can break away around a cut and need repair first.

  6. 06

    Free panel spaces

    New circuits need breaker spaces. A panel with none left, or an old fuse panel, brings panel work into the plan.

  7. 07

    The grounding connection

    Where the grounding conductor meets the water service, and whether that bond is present, tight and continuous back to the panel.

  8. 08

    Who is home during the work

    Bedrooms that must be usable each night, a home office, pets and medical equipment all shape the room order.

What comes first

  1. 1

    Stop: signs of heat

    Discolored plates, a warm box or a burning smell on original wiring move that circuit to the front of the queue. Highlights Chicago provides emergency service 24/7.

  3. 2

    First: heavy loads on original circuits

    Kitchen appliances, a laundry, window air conditioners or space heaters running on original wire move to new circuits first.

  5. 3

    Before the walls close

    A remodel's open walls set the date for those rooms. Once they are closed, the easiest route for new wire is gone.

  7. 4

    Planned: sound wiring on light loads

    Original wiring that tests sound and carries only lights can be scheduled in phases, or around the next remodel.

If there is smoke or flame, everyone leaves the building and calls 911 from outside. Nobody should open a box or handle original wire to look.

Rewire now, or later?

Original wiring is not a fault by itself, and a circuit that tests sound can stay in service.

  • Damaged or heat-baked insulation is the line: Chicago's minimum standards for existing buildings require known electrical hazards to be abated (14X-7-705.1).

  • The same chapter bars cube adapters and unfused plug strips (14X-7-705.2), so a home that runs on them needs more outlets or circuits.

  • Where insulation is sound and loads are light, the timing can follow a remodel or a sale.

The garage still on an old feed

A detached garage or coach house is easy to leave out of a rewire. Its feed may still be an old cable buried across the yard or strung overhead from the house. A run thought to be dead should be tested before anyone cuts it, since an old splice upstream can keep it live.

01Is a partial rewire enough, or does it leave old wire behind?

Often it is, when the original circuits that stay test sound and carry light loads. The risk sits at the boundary. Joins between new and original wire belong in boxes that stay accessible (NEC 314.29), not buried in a wall. The panel directory should then say which circuits are still original, so the next electrician is not guessing.

02Can a household keep living at home during a rewire?

Usually, yes. Most of the time, power is off only on the circuits being replaced. A panel or service change takes the whole home off for that swap. In homes built before 1978, painted surfaces can contain lead. The EPA requires anyone paid to disturb that paint to be certified in lead-safe work practices (epa.gov, read October 17, 2026). The household can ask how dust will be contained.

03Do insurers or buyers require knob-and-tube to be replaced?

That depends on the insurer or the deal. Chicago adopts the National Electrical Code's knob-and-tube article without changes (Municipal Code of Chicago 14E-3-394), and that article still recognizes existing installations. So a demand to replace working knob-and-tube comes from the insurer or the buyer, not from that code article.

04Does a rewire mean a bigger electrical service?

Not by itself. A rewire replaces branch circuits, while the service size follows the load. Chicago's minimum standards require the service to supply the calculated and actual loads under the Chicago Electrical Code (14X-7-702.1). Central air or an induction range added during a rewire can tip that math. A bigger service is panel work, and 281 of Highlights Chicago's 486 stand-alone electrical permits are panel or service work.

05Who can legally rewire a house in Chicago?

A business holding a City of Chicago electrical contractor license, with its own employees working under a licensed supervising electrician. The City says the license applies whether or not a job needs a permit. It does not cover workers paid as independent contractors (chicago.gov, read October 17, 2026). Highlights Chicago is a licensed and insured electrical contractor.

06What should a rewire quote spell out so two can be compared?

A comparable quote names which circuits get new wire and which stay original, and the wiring method in each area. It also covers the openings and who patches them, any panel or grounding work, the City permit and the payment terms. Two quotes that differ on those points are not pricing the same job. Why the age matters electrically

A building from 1906 was wired, if it was wired at all, for lighting. Everything since — the refrigerator, the window units, the home office, the induction range, the car — has been added to a service that was never designed for it. That is the single thread running through most Chicago electrical work.

Township by township

West Chicago is the oldest at 83% pre-1940 across 65,664 properties. Lake View follows at 71% across 23,046. Jefferson township — Portage Park, Jefferson Park, Irving Park — is younger at 60% pre-1940 but far larger at 112,923 properties, and its median year of 1927 puts most of it in the bungalow era. Rogers Park is the youngest of the north-side townships at a 1938 median.

What that means room by room

188,389 properties citywide are two-storey and 154,926 single-storey; 324,305 have full basements, which is where the panel usually lives and where access is usually straightforward. 107,336 properties have no garage at all — relevant the moment an EV charger enters the conversation. And 335,769 use a warm-air furnace against 98,849 on hot water or steam, which decides what kind of equipment circuits exist already.

Rewiring around walls, paint and people

Three questions decide how a rewire feels to live through. They cover what Chicago allows behind closed walls, the order of the trades, and what a grounding update changes.

When can new wire be fished through closed walls in Chicago?

When nothing else is taking the finished surface down. Chicago lets listed armored or metal-clad cable be fished into existing walls, floors and hard ceilings, in short runs between junction boxes. Once a remodel removes the finish, the wiring in that wall goes into raceway, such as conduit, instead. Customers describe Highlights Chicago jobs passing City inspection.

Older Chicago houses and flats were typically finished over wood or metal lath, often against brick. The Cook County Assessor dates 83.0% of the houses and two-to-six-unit buildings in West Chicago township to before 1940.

The common assumption is that a rewire means stripping rooms to the studs. That adds demolition, new wall finishes and weeks of dust to a home that people still live in.

Chicago's code writes a narrower path for existing homes. Its versions of NEC 320.10 and 330.10 let listed AC or MC cable, with listed fittings, be fished into existing walls, floors and hard ceilings. Each run between junction boxes is capped at 25 feet (Municipal Code of Chicago 14E-3-320 and 14E-3-330, read October 17, 2026). The permission holds only where other work does not remove the finished surface.

So one home can be wired both ways: fished cable where rooms stay finished, and conduit where a remodel opens the walls.

Seth T. (July 2023) describes a bathroom light moved by cutting the existing pipe and setting a new box. The debris from the wall cut was cleaned up afterward.

How is a rewire sequenced in a home people are living in?

Wiring first, then the City inspection where the job is permitted, then patching, then paint. In a lived-in home that order repeats room by room. A customer describes Boz, the owner of Highlights Chicago, telling the crew to keep wall damage down to spare extra painting (Tony, April 2026).

In a remodel, a rewire shares the house with other trades. A plasterer handles the cuts and a painter follows, sometimes beside a kitchen or bath crew. In a two-flat or three-flat, the neighbors upstairs share the dust and the noise.

The usual plan books every trade at once and hopes they meet in the middle. The painter arrives before the patches have cured, or a wall gets closed while a box still needs to move.

Treating the electrical openings as the spine of the schedule fixes that. Each room's cuts are final once its wiring is done and, where the permit calls for it, inspected. The plasterer can then patch that room while the electricians move on.

What does a grounding update involve in an older Chicago home?

It starts at the service: the grounding electrode conductor runs from the panel to the water pipe and to a supplemental electrode. Chicago adds its own rule for how that conductor is protected. The City's Express Permit terms require grounding and bonding deficiencies in the existing system to be corrected as part of permitted electrical work.

Older Chicago houses and flats were commonly grounded to the cold water pipe. A later plumbing repair can interrupt the metal path that old clamp relied on.

The usual rewire stops at the panel. New circuits get a proper equipment ground, while the old grounding electrode connection at the water pipe stays as it was.

The 2017 NEC that Chicago adopts requires a metal water pipe electrode to be supplemented by an additional electrode (250.53(D)(2)). Chicago's version of 250.64(B) also requires every grounding electrode conductor to run in RMC, IMC or EMT, all metal raceways. The source is Municipal Code of Chicago 14E-2-250, read October 17, 2026.
