Keeping a sump pump powered: three layers

A sump pump is only as reliable as the outlet it plugs into.

Most pump failures that reach an electrician are power failures: a tripped outlet nobody noticed, or a circuit shared with a freezer or a dehumidifier.

  • Its own circuit

    A pump on a dedicated circuit cannot be tripped by another appliance starting up on the same line.

  • Protection that is easy to see

    GFCI protection placed where a trip is noticed, such as a reset visible from the stairs, shortens the time a pump sits dead.

  • A second line of defense

    A high-water alarm or a backup pump on a separate circuit keeps working when the main pump's circuit trips.

Site assessment

What a new circuit depends on

Most of the price for this work is set by the panel, the route and the equipment label. These are the points that decide it.

  1. 01

    The equipment nameplate

    The label gives volts and watts or amps. The breaker and wire size are worked out from it, never guessed.

  2. 02

    Sizing for a storage heater

    Under the Chicago Electrical Code, which is based on the NEC, section 422.13 treats a storage water heater as a continuous load. Its circuit is rated at least 125% of the nameplate.

  3. 03

    Free spaces in the panel

    A 240-volt breaker takes two spaces side by side. A full panel changes the route to the equipment.

  4. 04

    Spare capacity on the service

    A load calculation under Article 220 of the electrical code shows whether the service can carry the new load on top of what the home already uses.

  5. 05

    The route from panel to equipment

    Open basement joists make a short, easy run. Finished ceilings, brick walls and a second floor add time.

  6. 06

    A way to shut it off

    The code calls for a disconnect within sight of the heater or pump, or a breaker that can be locked in the off position.

  7. 07

    Outlet protection by the pit

    Current electrical codes call for GFCI protection on most basement receptacles.

  8. 08

    Bonding at the heater

    Metal water piping has to stay tied into the grounding system. A heater swap is the moment to confirm that connection is intact.

  9. 09

    For a well: the run to the wellhead

    The distance, the burial depth and the pump's control box set the wire size and the length of the trench.

If the panel has no room for the circuit

  1. 1

    Compact breakers

    Where the panel is listed for them, compact breakers free a space for a 120-volt pump circuit.

  3. 2

    A subpanel

    A small subpanel near the laundry or utility room adds spaces when the service has capacity to spare.

  5. 3

    A panel or service upgrade

    When the service cannot carry the new load, or the panel is old and full, the upgrade comes before the heater circuit.

  7. 4

    A heater sized to the panel

    Some 120-volt heat pump water heaters avoid a new 240-volt circuit entirely, if the model is chosen with that in mind.

Which route fits depends on what the panel and the load calculation show for that home.

Repair, replace or rewire?

A tripped GFCI, a loose termination or a worn outlet is a repair, and the heater or pump stays.

  • A shorted element or a failed pressure switch is a part swap.

  • A heater or pump that keeps tripping a correctly sized circuit has usually reached the end of its life.

A new heater the old circuit cannot feed

A water heater swap can change the load. When the new unit needs a bigger circuit than the old one, or the old heater ran on gas, the existing wiring cannot simply be reused. The installation manual lists the circuit the new heater needs.

01Does switching to an electric or heat pump water heater mean a new panel?

Usually not. A tank or heat pump heater adds one circuit, and many Chicago panels have room for it. Tankless electric units are the exception, because several large circuits can exceed what a 100-amp service has spare. In that case the panel or service upgrade is priced as its own line, separate from the heater circuit.

02Will a generator keep the sump pump running in a power outage?

It can. A standby generator with a transfer switch can carry the sump pump circuit. The pump is one of the first loads worth putting on it. Highlights Chicago installs standby generators and their transfer switches. To feed house circuits, a portable generator needs an approved inlet with an interlock or transfer switch, never a cord plugged into a wall outlet.

03Can a new water heater use the old dryer outlet?

Not as a shortcut. A dryer receptacle belongs to the dryer, and most 240-volt water heaters connect directly to their own circuit. An unused dryer circuit can sometimes be repurposed when its breaker, wire size and route all suit the heater's nameplate. The heater's manual says which connection it needs.

04Is a sump pump that stopped during a storm an emergency?

Yes, when water is rising in the pit. If water is on the basement floor, nobody should walk in or touch the pump's plug; call an electrician. With the floor dry, the first check needs no tools: the pump's breaker at the panel, then the GFCI that feeds the pump.

05Can a sump pump and an ejector pump share one circuit?

It is better that they do not. If both pumps sit on one circuit, a single fault stops both, and two motors starting together can trip the breaker. Many pump manuals call for a dedicated circuit. Highlights Chicago can split them onto separate circuits where the panel has room, which also shows which pump caused a trip.

06Does a well pump need a different kind of contractor?

Partly. The pump inside the well, the pipe and the pressure tank are work for a licensed well and pump contractor. The circuit to the well and its disconnect are electrical work, and the pressure switch and control box wiring is often shared between the two trades. Highlights Chicago handles the electrical side as a City of Chicago electrical contractor.

07Does a heat pump water heater work in a cold Chicago basement?

It works, with a catch. A heat pump water heater draws heat from the air around it and cools the room as it runs. In a cold basement it leans more on its backup element, which draws as much as a standard tank. That is why most 240-volt models still get a full-size circuit.

We work citywide, with the largest share of our permit record in Wards 1, 32, 26, 47 and 27 on the north and northwest sides. We also hold current registration in Evanston and Palatine.

Why the age matters electrically

A building from 1906 was wired, if it was wired at all, for lighting. Everything since — the refrigerator, the window units, the home office, the induction range, the car — has been added to a service that was never designed for it. That is the single thread running through most Chicago electrical work.

Township by township

West Chicago is the oldest at 83% pre-1940 across 65,664 properties. Lake View follows at 71% across 23,046. Jefferson township — Portage Park, Jefferson Park, Irving Park — is younger at 60% pre-1940 but far larger at 112,923 properties, and its median year of 1927 puts most of it in the bungalow era. Rogers Park is the youngest of the north-side townships at a 1938 median.

What that means room by room

188,389 properties citywide are two-storey and 154,926 single-storey; 324,305 have full basements, which is where the panel usually lives and where access is usually straightforward. 107,336 properties have no garage at all — relevant the moment an EV charger enters the conversation. And 335,769 use a warm-air furnace against 98,849 on hot water or steam, which decides what kind of equipment circuits exist already.

Guide 04 / 04

How water heaters and pumps fit a Chicago electrical system

Hot water and drainage are plumbing, but both depend on circuits. In Chicago that mostly means water heaters and basement pumps, and only rarely a well.

What does a well pump circuit involve?

Chicago draws its drinking water from Lake Michigan, so a private well inside the city is rare. Wells are more common on land outside a municipal water system.

Most submersible well pumps run on 230 volts from a double-pole breaker, though smaller pumps can run on 115 volts. The breaker and wire size come from the pump maker's instructions and the motor nameplate. A 2-wire pump keeps its starting parts inside the motor. A 3-wire pump needs a control box at the surface, holding the start capacitor and relay.

The run to the wellhead is often long, so the wire is sized for voltage drop as well as current. Underground wiring is buried at the depth the code requires, and an equipment ground is carried to the pump. A disconnect lets the pump be shut off for service. Constant-pressure systems add a variable-speed drive with its own wiring and grounding instructions.

Why do so many Chicago basements depend on a pump?

Much of Chicago was built on flat, low ground, and many basements sit below the level of the sewer they drain to. A basement bathroom or laundry in such a home drains through an ejector pump that lifts the waste up to the sewer line.

Groundwater adds a second pump in many homes. A sump pit collects water from around the foundation, and the pump pushes it out before it reaches the floor. Both pumps run on house power, so a tripped circuit can mean a wet basement.

What does the move away from gas mean for a water heater circuit?

A gas water heater needs little or no electricity. A switch to an electric tank or a heat pump model adds a large new load that the panel has to carry.

That matters in a city of older homes. The Cook County Assessor dates 58.6% of Chicago's single-family and 2 to 6 unit buildings to before 1940. In the City's service and panel upgrade permits for the 24 months to September 2026, 1,401 listed a new 100-amp service and 2,038 a new 200-amp service. A 100-amp service is still being installed in Chicago.

The heater's installation manual lists the circuit it needs, and the panel label shows what is already there. Highlights Chicago wires electric tank and heat pump heaters, and those two labels read together show whether a home needs panel work first.
