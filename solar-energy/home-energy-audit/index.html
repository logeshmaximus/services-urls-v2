Room for a new electric load: three routes

A full-looking panel is not the same as a full service.

Whether a heat pump, range or charger fits is a matter of load math, not the panel's age. The library tab works through the heat pump case.

  • Fit it on the service as it is

    Where the numbers leave room, the new circuit takes a free space in the panel. Chicago's code bars tandem breakers (14E-2-240).

  • Manage the charger load

    Where an automatic load management system limits charging, 2017 NEC 625.42 counts that system's maximum load on the service and feeder.

  • Upgrade the service

    When the numbers leave no room, a larger service is the plain answer. The pricing block shows what Chicago permits record for that work.

Which route fits depends on the measured demand, the equipment ratings and the panel listing. Chicago adopts NEC Articles 220 and 625 unchanged (Municipal Code of Chicago 14E-2-220 and 14E-6-625, read October 19, 2026).

Site assessment

What an electrical assessment checks

These checks, made at the meter, the panel and the planned equipment, decide whether an energy plan needs a circuit, a panel or a new service.

  1. 01

    The service rating

    The main breaker and the meter base set the ceiling for every new electric load in the building.

  2. 02

    A year of real usage

    The account's usage history shows the building's real peak demand.

  3. 03

    Free spaces in the panel

    A 240-volt appliance takes a two-pole breaker, which usually needs two spaces. A full panel changes the route.

  4. 04

    Large loads already wired

    Electric water heaters, dryers, ranges and window air conditioners are listed with their ratings before anything is added.

  5. 05

    Signs of heat at the panel

    Discolored breakers, a warm cover or a burnt smell point to a loose or overloaded connection, which comes before any new load.

  6. 06

    The route to each new appliance

    An outdoor heat pump pad, a kitchen range or a garage charger each sets a run length and a way through walls.

  7. 07

    Lighting hours, room by room

    Fixtures that burn longest are where LED pays back first, so the count starts with kitchens, halls and common areas.

  8. 08

    House meter in a flat building

    In a two-flat or larger, the house meter feeds halls, a boiler or shared laundry, and it is read on its own.

Which comes first: the envelope or the wiring?

  1. 1

    Sealing and insulation first

    Lower heat loss can mean a smaller heat pump, and a smaller heat pump can mean a smaller circuit.

  3. 2

    Electrical corrections first

    A panel showing heat damage, or one with no room, is put right before new loads are planned around it.

  5. 3

    Both on one schedule

    The auditor's report and the electrical work are planned together, so neither trade waits on the other.

Which order fits depends on the assessment report and on what the panel shows.

Repair, add a circuit, or upgrade?

Many energy symptoms end without a new service.

  • A space heater that trips a breaker needs its own circuit or a different heat source.

  • A service upgrade comes in when the measured demand cannot carry the new loads, or when the panel itself is damaged or obsolete.

01Does a home need a paid energy audit before electrical upgrades?

No. A single new circuit, such as one for an induction range, can be planned from the panel, the bills and the appliance's rating. A full assessment pays off most when the heating is changing, because it looks at the whole house, not one appliance. The next answer covers how to get one in Chicago.

02How does a Chicago household get an energy assessment, and what does it cost?

Through the utilities it can cost nothing. ComEd offers qualifying customers a free assessment with free products, booked online or at 1-855-433-2700; an in-home visit depends on eligibility, including household income. Peoples Gas books free in-person or virtual assessments for owners of single-family homes, two-flats and individually metered condos (both utilities' pages, read October 17, 2026). A private certified auditor sets its own fee.

03Is a warm panel or a burning smell an emergency?

Treat it as one. Leave the panel closed, turn off large appliances, and call 911 if there is smoke, flame or sparking. A cover too hot to touch or a smell of burning plastic should not wait for a planned assessment. Highlights Chicago offers emergency electrical service 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

04Do LED bulbs alone make a big difference to a Chicago electric bill?

LED bulbs help most where lights burn for long hours, such as halls, kitchens and outdoor fixtures. Peoples Gas notes that appliances, space heating and water heating make up a large portion of a home's energy use (Peoples Gas Home Savings page, read October 17, 2026). Lighting is one part of that picture.

05What circuit does a heat pump or induction range need?

Each usually gets its own 240-volt circuit. A heat pump's nameplate lists a minimum circuit ampacity and a maximum breaker size. 2017 NEC 440.14 calls for a disconnect within sight of the outdoor unit. An induction range is sized from the rating in its installation manual. Those ratings, not a rule of thumb, go into the load math.

06Does Highlights Chicago carry out the energy audit itself?

A full energy audit, with a blower door test and an insulation survey, is a certified auditor's job or part of a utility assessment. Highlights Chicago is a City-licensed electrical contractor, and the electrical items an audit turns up, from new circuits to panel work, are licensed electrical work.

07Will going from gas to a heat pump or induction raise the electric bill?

The electric bill usually rises while the gas bill falls. The net change depends on the home's heat loss, the equipment's efficiency and both utilities' rates, which change over time. The comparison is best run on current rates for both fuels, not on an old rule of thumb or a neighbor's numbers.

08Is a whole-home energy monitor worth having wired in?

It can show which loads drive the bill, hour by hour. Many whole-home monitors clip sensors around the main feeders inside the panel, so installing one means opening live equipment, which is not a homeowner job. A smart thermostat is simpler, and Peoples Gas lists it among the free or discounted products in its assessment.

09Is there any discount on the electrical work?

Highlights Chicago offers a military discount, and it accepts credit cards, Zelle, Venmo, PayPal, Cash App and cryptocurrency. Utility rebates on qualifying equipment are a separate matter. Each program sets its own rules, and the pricing notes name the official pages read for this page.

10Do you cover my part of Chicago?

We work citywide, with the largest share of our permit record in Wards 1, 32, 26, 47 and 27 on the north and northwest sides. We also hold current registration in Evanston and Palatine.

Why the age matters electrically

A building from 1906 was wired, if it was wired at all, for lighting. Everything since — the refrigerator, the window units, the home office, the induction range, the car — has been added to a service that was never designed for it. That is the single thread running through most Chicago electrical work.

Township by township

West Chicago is the oldest at 83% pre-1940 across 65,664 properties. Lake View follows at 71% across 23,046. Jefferson township — Portage Park, Jefferson Park, Irving Park — is younger at 60% pre-1940 but far larger at 112,923 properties, and its median year of 1927 puts most of it in the bungalow era. Rogers Park is the youngest of the north-side townships at a 1938 median.

What that means room by room

188,389 properties citywide are two-storey and 154,926 single-storey; 324,305 have full basements, which is where the panel usually lives and where access is usually straightforward. 107,336 properties have no garage at all — relevant the moment an EV charger enters the conversation. And 335,769 use a warm-air furnace against 98,849 on hot water or steam, which decides what kind of equipment circuits exist already.

The electrical side of a Chicago energy plan

An energy plan is written by one trade and carried out by several. In Chicago's older buildings, the electrical part can decide how far the plan goes.

What should a household do with the electrical items in a ComEd or Peoples Gas assessment report?

Sort the report items into three piles. Products the program installs itself need no electrician. Envelope work, such as sealing and insulation, has its own trades. Items that add a load, such as a heat pump, an electric water heater or a charger, become wiring work. Highlights Chicago is a contractor for that third pile.

Peoples Gas describes its assessment as free and discounted products plus recommendations for upgrades that qualify for rebates (Peoples Gas Home Energy Savings page, read October 17, 2026). In ComEd's program, eligible households can then receive weatherization from a qualifying service provider or community action agency. Under the former 25C credit, a qualified auditor held a certification from a program on a Department of Energy list (IRS page, read October 17, 2026).

The usual reading treats the recommendations as one shopping list. Equipment gets bought in the order it appears, and the panel is first looked at when the installer arrives.

The items do not share one trade or one timeline. A program product goes in during the visit, while a heat pump waits on a load check and, where one is needed, a City permit. Pricing the wiring items from the report before equipment is bought keeps a panel surprise out of the schedule.

Highlights Chicago's part of such a report is its wiring items: circuits, panel work and lighting. Customers describe Highlights Chicago jobs passing City inspection.

Does switching from gas heat to a heat pump mean a bigger service in a Chicago home?

Not always. The deciding numbers are the heat pump's minimum circuit ampacity and any backup electric heat strips, which can be the largest load the switch adds. Where the service still runs short, a service change follows, and Highlights Chicago's electrical panel upgrade page covers that step.

By Cook County Assessor records for tax year 2025, 45.6% of the city's single-family houses went up before 1940, long before electric heat pumps.

The usual quote for going electric starts with a 200-amp upgrade. Its standard load calculation can come out higher than any demand the building has recorded.

In a heat pump switch, the numbers that matter are on the equipment. The outdoor unit's nameplate gives its minimum circuit ampacity. Backup heat strips in the air handler can draw more than the compressor, and on the coldest nights both run. Section 220.87 of the 2017 NEC, the base of Chicago's Electrical Code, lets a year of measured peak demand stand in for the existing load. ComEd's smart meters record usage in short intervals, which can show that peak.

Highlights Chicago's stand-alone electrical permit record has no separate heat pump category. Most of it is panel and service work, which is where a heat pump that outgrows the service leads. For that step, the City's Express Permit Program lists "New electrical service or service disconnecting means" as a subtype.

Eunice K. wrote that a Highlights Chicago technician found and fixed an electric baseboard heater that had stopped working (December 2025).

What does a benchmarking report or utility study mean for the wiring in a Chicago commercial building?

Large Chicago buildings must report energy use to the City every year. Findings on lighting, controls and wiring, from that report or from a utility study, then become electrical work. Highlights Chicago's reviews include office and salon lighting and standing service for retail stores.

Covered buildings, 50,000 square feet and above, report energy use to the City by June 1 every year. The data is verified every three years, under Chapter 18-14 of the Municipal Code (City Energy Benchmarking page, read October 17, 2026).

A score arrives, and a lighting vendor proposes a fixture-for-fixture swap. Fixture counts drive that quote, while hours of use, controls and the circuits feeding common areas go unexamined.

Savings follow hours. A hallway or garage light that never switches off can save as much from a sensor or a schedule as from a new fixture.
