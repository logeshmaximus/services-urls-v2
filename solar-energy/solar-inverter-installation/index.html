Replacing a failed inverter: three routes

A failed inverter does not mean a new solar system.

The panels usually outlast the inverter. What changes the price is whether the same model, or one that fits the same wiring, is still made.

  • Same model, same place

    When the model is still sold, the swap reuses the mounting, the wiring and the disconnects.

  • A compatible model

    When it is discontinued, a model that matches the array's voltage and current can often reuse most of the wiring.

  • A change of type

    Moving to microinverters or a hybrid inverter changes the roof work and the permit, not just the box on the wall.

Site assessment

What the site assessment checks on an inverter job

Most of the price is decided by what is already on the wall and in the panel. This is what gets checked, and why.

  1. 01

    Busbar and main breaker rating

    NEC 705.12 limits how much solar feed a panel busbar can carry. Older 100-amp panels in Chicago two-flats are often the limit.

  2. 02

    Space for the solar breaker

    A full panel needs a supply-side tap or a new panel before the inverter can connect.

  3. 03

    Inverter location

    The wall near the meter or panel sets the wire run and decides whether the inverter sits in direct sun or weather.

  4. 04

    Wire path from roof to panel

    Brick walls and flat roofs decide how conduit gets from the array down to the inverter and the panel.

  5. 05

    Grounding and bonding

    The array frame, the inverter and the panel need one continuous ground path back to the service.

  6. 06

    Rapid shutdown

    NEC 690.12 requires roof-level shutdown on most systems. Older systems can predate the rule, which matters when equipment changes.

  7. 07

    The meter and the service connection

    The meter the inverter feeds and the service it ties into are checked, because a different inverter can change the interconnection.

If the panel can't take the inverter output

  1. 1

    A different breaker size

    Where the load calculation allows it, a smaller main or solar breaker brings the panel within the busbar limit.

  3. 2

    A supply-side connection

    A tap between the meter and the main breaker avoids loading the busbar at all, where the code path for the building allows it.

  5. 3

    A panel or service upgrade

    When the panel is old or full anyway, an upgrade solves the solar connection and the capacity problem together.

Which route fits depends on the busbar rating, the free breaker spaces and the load calculation.

01Does a failed inverter mean the whole solar system needs replacing?

No. A failed inverter is usually replaced on its own. A tripped breaker, a blown fuse or a loose connection can also look like a dead inverter. The error history and the wiring are worth checking before a new unit is bought. A replacement makes sense once the fault is confirmed.

02Can a different brand replace the old inverter?

Often, yes. The new inverter has to match the array's voltage and current range and fit the existing disconnects, or the wiring changes too. Switching from a string inverter to microinverters also means work on the roof, so it is a bigger job than a swap.

03Does replacing an inverter need a City permit?

Yes. In Chicago, replacing electrical equipment such as an inverter is permitted electrical work. The permit is taken out by a licensed electrical contractor such as Highlights Chicago. A City electrical inspector checks the finished job. A system changed without a permit can cause trouble at a home sale or an insurance claim.

04Does ComEd need to approve a new inverter?

ComEd approves every system that sends power to its grid. When the size or type of inverter changes, the interconnection rules can require an updated application before the system runs again. The new make and model are confirmed at the estimate so that paperwork is right.

05Will the solar panels keep the lights on in a power outage?

Not on their own. A grid-tied inverter shuts off when ComEd's power goes out, so line workers are not exposed to power from the roof. Backup needs a battery-ready inverter or a battery with a backup panel, which Highlights Chicago prices as a separate part of the job.

06Does a new inverter work with the existing monitoring app?

Monitoring is tied to the inverter maker. A same-brand replacement can usually join the existing account, while a new brand means a new app and a new login. Keeping the old login details before the swap saves the system history, which otherwise stays with the old account.

07Is a dead inverter an emergency?

Usually not. A dead inverter stops production but is not a hazard by itself. Smoke or flames at electrical equipment call for 911 first. A burning smell, a scorched disconnect or a solar breaker that will not reset is also urgent. Highlights Chicago offers emergency electrical service 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

08Do you cover my part of Chicago?

We work citywide, with the largest share of our permit record in Wards 1, 32, 26, 47 and 27 on the north and northwest sides. We also hold current registration in Evanston and Palatine.

Why the age matters electrically

A building from 1906 was wired, if it was wired at all, for lighting. Everything since — the refrigerator, the window units, the home office, the induction range, the car — has been added to a service that was never designed for it. That is the single thread running through most Chicago electrical work.

Township by township

West Chicago is the oldest at 83% pre-1940 across 65,664 properties. Lake View follows at 71% across 23,046. Jefferson township — Portage Park, Jefferson Park, Irving Park — is younger at 60% pre-1940 but far larger at 112,923 properties, and its median year of 1927 puts most of it in the bungalow era. Rogers Park is the youngest of the north-side townships at a 1938 median.

What that means room by room

188,389 properties citywide are two-storey and 154,926 single-storey; 324,305 have full basements, which is where the panel usually lives and where access is usually straightforward. 107,336 properties have no garage at all — relevant the moment an EV charger enters the conversation. And 335,769 use a warm-air furnace against 98,849 on hot water or steam, which decides what kind of equipment circuits exist already.

Guide 04 / 04

How an inverter fits a Chicago electrical system

The inverter turns the direct current from the panels into the alternating current the house uses. It is also the part of a solar system most likely to need an electrician.

Will a new inverter change how much the system produces?

It can, in either direction. An inverter is sized against the array. The ratio between the panels' rated output and the inverter's rating decides how often it limits production on bright days.

A like-for-like swap keeps that ratio. A model with a lower rating can trim the sunniest hours. A slightly higher rating rarely adds much, because the panels set the ceiling.

The fair test is the first months of monitoring after the swap, compared with the same months the year before. Weather and dirt on the panels move the numbers too, so one bright week proves little.

How common is solar work on Chicago permits?

Common. The City runs a dedicated Express Permit Program route for small-scale solar PV systems, and solar and battery storage is one of the larger categories in its permit data.

Many of those systems will need an inverter replaced during their life. On a microinverter system, that usually means one unit on the roof at a time, not the whole set.

The replacement is electrical work: the inverter, its disconnects and the connection to the panel the inverter feeds.

What makes a two-flat or condo system different?

A two-flat or condo building has a meter bank, one meter for each unit and often a house meter for the common areas. The solar system connects behind one of those meters, and the choice decides whose bill the solar reduces.

The usual mistake is to connect to whichever panel is closest. In a condo building that can put one owner's solar on the building's common meter.
