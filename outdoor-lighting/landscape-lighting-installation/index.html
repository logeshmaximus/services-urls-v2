Three ways to switch a yard system on

A dial timer set in June turns the lights on long after dark by December.

A plug-in clock also resets after a power cut. Many transformers take one of the controls below, built in or added.

  • Photocell

    A light sensor that switches the system on at dusk, if it sees open sky rather than a porch light. Many pair it with an hours-on setting.

  • Astronomic timer

    Works out sunset from the date and location, so the schedule follows the season with no sensor to shade or bury in snow.

  • App or smart control

    Built into some transformers or added as a module. It needs Wi-Fi that reaches the transformer, often weak at a garage.

Site assessment

What the site check covers on a yard lighting job

The fixtures are the easy part. Where the power comes from, the route across the lot and what lies under the lawn decide the work.

  1. 01

    An outdoor outlet for the transformer

    A plug-in transformer needs a 120-volt receptacle outside or in the garage. The 2017 NEC asks for GFCI protection on both (210.8(A)(2) and (A)(3)), and a wet spot needs an extra-duty hood that closes over the plug.

  2. 02

    Room on the circuit

    A transformer draws a steady load for hours each night. Article 411 limits the supply to a branch circuit of 20 amps at most, and the circuit has to carry its other loads too.

  3. 03

    Transformer size

    The fixtures' total watts set the transformer size, with room left for later additions. A unit run near its limit runs hot and can shut itself off.

  4. 04

    How deep the cable goes

    Table 300.5 of the 2017 NEC sets 6 inches of cover for irrigation and landscape lighting circuits of not more than 30 volts in Type UF or other identified cable or raceway. Depths for 120-volt runs are on the outdoor lighting page.

  5. 05

    Water features nearby

    Article 411 keeps low-voltage lighting 10 feet back from the edge of a pool, spa or fountain unless Article 680 allows it.

  6. 06

    Where the cable enters a building

    Article 411 lets a low-voltage cable pass through a wall only in a Chapter 3 wiring method or on a listed Class 2 supply. Yard cable from a transformer that is not a listed Class 2 supply, pushed through a gap in the brick, does not qualify.

Where the transformer gets its power

  1. 1

    An outdoor GFCI outlet that is already there

    When one sits near the beds and its circuit has room, the transformer plugs in there.

  3. 2

    A new outdoor outlet on a nearby circuit

    Added through the wall from a circuit with spare capacity, with GFCI protection and an extra-duty hood.

  5. 3

    A new circuit from the panel

    When no nearby circuit has room, or the system is large.

  7. 4

    A hardwired transformer

    Some transformers connect straight to a 120-volt circuit in a box instead of plugging in, often on a garage wall.

The route is settled once the outlets, the circuits with room and the distance to the beds are known. Chicago amends Article 411 only at 411.3 and leaves the Table 300.5 cover depths unchanged (Municipal Code of Chicago, Title 14E, read October 19, 2026).

Repair the run, or replace the system?

A dark run usually comes back with a repair, not a new system.

  • A cut cable gets a waterproof splice listed for burial.

  • A corroded connection is remade, and a failed transformer or photocell is swapped on its own.

  • Replacement makes sense when old fixtures have rusted through or the layout no longer fits the yard.

A system with no installer left

Many yard systems were put in by a landscaping crew or a past owner, with no drawing of where the cable runs. Tracing starts at the transformer and follows each run. A cable locator finds a line that no utility will mark, since utilities mark only their own.

01Does yard lighting need a new circuit or a bigger panel?

Rarely a bigger panel. An LED path light commonly draws only a few watts. A whole yard system often runs from one transformer plugged into an existing outdoor GFCI outlet. A new outlet or circuit comes in only when none is near the beds or the nearest circuit is full. Highlights Chicago installs yard systems in both cases.

02Is 12-volt yard lighting safe around children and pets?

Largely, at the fixture end. At 12 volts, a nicked cable or an open fixture carries little shock risk for a child or a dog. The risk that remains is at the transformer's plug-in end, which the GFCI outlet guards. Loose or overloaded connections can still run hot, so a damaged fixture is best left switched off.

03Why are the last lights on a run dimmer than the first?

Voltage drop. A 12-volt system loses a little voltage along each foot of cable, so the farthest fixtures get less. Heavier cable or a 13 to 15-volt tap on a multi-tap transformer evens it out, and the library tab covers layouts that shorten the runs. LED fixtures show the drop less than halogen.

04Is a store-bought yard lighting kit good enough?

For a few path lights near an existing outdoor outlet, a listed kit can work. Kits tend to go wrong in three places. The transformer is too small for later additions, the cable is left on the surface where a spade finds it, and splices let water in. Highlights Chicago can wire the outdoor GFCI outlet a kit plugs into.

05How many lights does a small yard need?

Fewer than most kits suggest. A few path lights where the walk turns or changes level often do the job. Add one or two uplights on the main tree or the house front, and step lights where there are stairs. Even rows of path lights at a fixed spacing tend to look like a runway. Marking the spots with a flashlight at dusk is a good start.

06Can yard lighting share a trench with a garage feed or EV charger line?

Often the same route works. The deeper power feed goes in first, and the low-voltage cable is kept apart from the power conductors as the code requires. Planning both at once saves digging the yard twice. Highlights Chicago also wires EV chargers, so a garage charger and the yard lights can be priced together.

07How do yard lights cope with a Chicago winter?

The low fixtures take the hits. Path lights get buried, clipped by plows or shoveled over, and salt corrodes housings beside the walk. Path lights set back from a shoveled edge, brass, copper or composite housings that resist salt corrosion, and splices kept out of low spots where meltwater sits all help. A photocell under snow can switch the lights on in daylight.

08Do you cover my part of Chicago?

We work citywide, with the largest share of our permit record in Wards 1, 32, 26, 47 and 27 on the north and northwest sides. We also hold current registration in Evanston and Palatine.

Why the age matters electrically

A building from 1906 was wired, if it was wired at all, for lighting. Everything since — the refrigerator, the window units, the home office, the induction range, the car — has been added to a service that was never designed for it. That is the single thread running through most Chicago electrical work.

Township by township

West Chicago is the oldest at 83% pre-1940 across 65,664 properties. Lake View follows at 71% across 23,046. Jefferson township — Portage Park, Jefferson Park, Irving Park — is younger at 60% pre-1940 but far larger at 112,923 properties, and its median year of 1927 puts most of it in the bungalow era. Rogers Park is the youngest of the north-side townships at a 1938 median.

What that means room by room

188,389 properties citywide are two-storey and 154,926 single-storey; 324,305 have full basements, which is where the panel usually lives and where access is usually straightforward. 107,336 properties have no garage at all — relevant the moment an EV charger enters the conversation. And 335,769 use a warm-air furnace against 98,849 on hot water or steam, which decides what kind of equipment circuits exist already.

Three questions the blocks above leave open. Which yard jobs need a City permit? How does an old halogen system move to LED? And how is a system laid out on a narrow Chicago lot?

Do low-voltage yard lights need a City permit in Chicago?

Sometimes. The City needs no permit for a low voltage fixture in any building. Low voltage wiring needs none inside a residential building of up to 4 stories above grade, or its garage. A cable buried across the yard falls outside that wording, so it may need one. Customers describe Highlights Chicago jobs passing City inspection where a permit was filed.

Chicago writes its own electrical code, Title 14E, on top of the 2017 NEC. Article 411 of that code covers lighting systems at 30 volts or less, the class a 12-volt yard system falls into.

Yard lighting is often sold as a weekend add-on: a kit transformer plugged into whatever outlet is close, and cable laid by a planting crew. The permit question rarely comes up until a fault or a sale.

The City's Express Permit instructions list Lighting among the low voltage systems that can be permitted. Low-voltage and decorative lighting stay out of the site lighting subtype for parking lots (chicago.gov, read October 17, 2026).

The same instructions say who may file. A general electrical contractor is needed for most electrical work, and a low voltage contractor may do work limited to low voltage. Highlights Chicago holds City of Chicago electrical contractor licenses ECC96456 and ECC94521.

Both licenses show as active on the City of Chicago's licensed contractor register, checked October 2, 2026.

How does an old halogen yard system move to LED?

Fixture by fixture, then the transformer. Many halogen fixtures take LED lamps, or the maker sells an LED module for the same housing. The transformer is checked last, because an LED load is far lighter and some older units misbehave under it. Highlights Chicago gives free estimates, so a relamp and a full replacement can be priced side by side.

Yard systems put in before LED fixtures caught on often still run halogen lamps. Those lamps run hot, draw several times the watts of an LED, and burn out every few seasons.

The usual fix is to swap lamps as they fail, one brand at a time. The yard ends up with mixed colors and mixed brightness, and the original transformer keeps running at a fraction of its rating.

LED changes the sums. The total load drops, so one transformer can carry more fixtures or longer runs. But some magnetic and electronic transformers flicker or will not start below a minimum load, and the label says which kind is on the wall.

Where a retrofit adds new yard cable that needs a City permit, Highlights Chicago is the contractor of record on the City permit.

How is yard lighting laid out on a narrow Chicago lot?

Short runs and aimed beams. On a narrow lot, one long chain of fixtures dims at the far end, and a wide beam lands on the next house. Feeding small groups from one point and narrowing the uplights fixes both.

A Chicago yard is often a strip between the house, a side fence and the back of the lot. Neighbors' windows can sit a few feet past the property line.

Kit layouts often string the fixtures on one cable from the transformer, like beads. On a deep lot the last lights run dim, and uplights chosen for brightness throw past their tree and into a bedroom next door.

A hub layout runs one cable to a central point and branches to a few fixtures from there, so each sees about the same voltage. Narrow-beam uplights, glare shields and aiming away from the property line keep the light in the yard.
