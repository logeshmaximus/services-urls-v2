15-amp, 20-amp, USB or smart: which receptacle?

All four mount in a standard single-gang opening, though USB and smart devices need more box depth. NEC 210.21(B) sets which receptacle ratings belong on which circuit.

240-volt outlets: dryer, range or car

A 240-volt outlet is a new circuit, not a device swap.

Each one takes a two-pole breaker and wire sized to the appliance. The nameplate on the dryer, range or charger sets that size.

  • Dryer outlet

    Usually a 30-amp circuit with a four-slot NEMA 14-30 receptacle. Under NEC 250.140, an old three-slot dryer outlet may stay on its existing circuit under set conditions; a new dryer circuit is four-wire.

  • Range outlet

    Usually a 40- or 50-amp circuit with a four-slot NEMA 14-50 receptacle behind the range. The range's nameplate rating decides which size.

  • Plug-in car charging

    A NEMA 14-50 or 6-50 receptacle on its own circuit. NEC 625.41 sizes that circuit at 125 percent of the charger's continuous draw, so a 40-amp charger needs a 50-amp circuit.

What gets checked before outlets are added

These checks decide whether an existing circuit can take an added outlet or a new circuit is needed.

Illustrative photo

  1. 01

    Load already on the circuit

    A space heater, a microwave or a window air conditioner can already use most of a circuit. The City's Express Permit terms make the contractor on the permit responsible for that capacity.

  2. 02

    Breaker matched to wire

    A breaker larger than the wire it protects turns up in older panels. It has to be corrected before anything is added to that circuit.

  3. 03

    Free panel spaces

    A new 120-volt circuit needs one space in the panel, and a 240-volt circuit needs two.

  4. 04

    A path from the panel

    Basement ceilings, closets stacked floor over floor and attics decide how far a run travels before it reaches the room.

  5. 05

    What the wall is made of

    The wall type, plaster, brick or drywall, decides the route and the patching.

  6. 06

    Box depth

    USB, smart and GFCI devices have deeper bodies than a plain outlet. NEC 314.16 caps how much a box may hold, and many old boxes are shallow.

  7. 07

    A ground path

    A tester shows whether the box has a ground wire or a metal conduit path back to the panel. Two-prong outlets with no ground follow their own rules, set out on the GFCI outlet page.

If the circuit cannot take more

  1. 1

Replace the outlet, or look at the wiring?

Most worn outlets are a device swap: the contacts wear out while the box and wire stay sound.

  • The wiring becomes the job when the same outlet scorches again after a new device, when outlets fail one after another along a run, or when insulation cracks as the wire is folded back into the box.

  • Then the run toward the panel is traced, and part of it may need new wire.

Outlets an earlier owner added

Older buildings sometimes carry outlets added without a permit: a cord stapled along a baseboard, or a basement outlet tapped off the nearest light. Each is traced to its source, then brought up to code or taken out. The City's Express Permit terms ask for existing deficiencies to be corrected as part of permitted work.

Incentives

No federal tax credit covers new outlets today. The IRS Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit covers improvements made through December 31, 2025 (irs.gov/credits-deductions/energy-efficient-home-improvement-credit). 01Does adding outlets to an older Chicago flat mean rewiring the whole unit?

Usually not. Outlets can often go on an existing circuit with spare capacity, or on one new circuit run from the panel. A rewire is the answer only when the old wire itself is failing along several runs. Highlights Chicago's electrical wiring and rewiring service covers that case when it comes up.

02How high should a new outlet sit on the wall?

The NEC sets no minimum height for ordinary wall outlets, though one more than 5½ feet up does not count toward the spacing rule. The counter is the exception: under 210.52(C)(5) counter outlets sit no more than 20 inches above the countertop. Highlights Chicago can match the height of the outlets already in a room, or set new ones to suit the furniture.

03Can an outlet be added behind a wall-mounted TV?

Yes. A recessed outlet box lets the plug sit flat behind the screen, fed from a circuit with room or from a new one. Running the TV's own power cord inside the wall down to a lower outlet is not allowed, so the outlet goes where the TV hangs. Highlights Chicago can set that box before the mount goes up.

04Will a USB outlet charge a laptop?

Sometimes. Many USB receptacles charge phones and tablets well, while only higher-output USB-C models suit a laptop, and the output is printed on the device. The plug slots on the same outlet still take a laptop's own charger. Highlights Chicago can install either kind of USB receptacle where a desk or nightstand needs one.

05Do new outlets in Chicago need arc-fault protection?

Often, yes. Chicago's amended 210.12 is not a room list. Every 120-volt, 15- and 20-amp dwelling-unit branch circuit needs combination-type AFCI, including circuits that are modified, replaced or extended. Protection goes at the circuit's origin or at its first outlet. AFCI is not required where the circuit runs in metal conduit, EMT or MI cable with metal boxes (Municipal Code of Chicago 14E-2-210, read October 19, 2026).

06What do new kitchen counter outlets need in Chicago?

Under the 2017 NEC, counter outlets run on at least two 20-amp small-appliance circuits (210.52(B)). Section 210.8(A) also calls for GFCI protection at counters, baths, garages, unfinished basements and outdoors; the GFCI outlet page covers it. Chicago's section 14E-2-210 does not amend 210.8(A) or 210.52(B), so both apply as written.

07Do you cover my part of Chicago?

We work citywide, with the largest share of our permit record in Wards 1, 32, 26, 47 and 27 on the north and northwest sides. We also hold current registration in Evanston and Palatine.

Why the age matters electrically

A building from 1906 was wired, if it was wired at all, for lighting. Everything since — the refrigerator, the window units, the home office, the induction range, the car — has been added to a service that was never designed for it. That is the single thread running through most Chicago electrical work.

Township by township

West Chicago is the oldest at 83% pre-1940 across 65,664 properties. Lake View follows at 71% across 23,046. Jefferson township — Portage Park, Jefferson Park, Irving Park — is younger at 60% pre-1940 but far larger at 112,923 properties, and its median year of 1927 puts most of it in the bungalow era. Rogers Park is the youngest of the north-side townships at a 1938 median.

What that means room by room

188,389 properties citywide are two-storey and 154,926 single-storey; 324,305 have full basements, which is where the panel usually lives and where access is usually straightforward. 107,336 properties have no garage at all — relevant the moment an EV charger enters the conversation. And 335,769 use a warm-air furnace against 98,849 on hot water or steam, which decides what kind of equipment circuits exist already.

Outlets in older Chicago walls: spacing, routes and safer devices

Three questions come up when outlets are added to a Chicago building that is older than everything plugged into it.

Why do so many prewar Chicago flats have so few outlets, and how many should a room have?

Most were wired for a ceiling light and a lamp, long before desks, TV walls and counter appliances. Chicago's electrical code builds on the 2017 NEC. It asks for outlets so that no point along a wall's floor line is more than 6 feet from one. Highlights Chicago answers both halves for an older flat: where the outlets go, and whether a circuit can carry them.

Cook County Assessor data for tax year 2025 date 58.6% of the city's houses and two- to six-unit buildings to before 1940. Rooms in those buildings were laid out for far less than a household plugs in today.

The common stopgap is a power strip, a cube tap or an extension cord under a rug. NEC 400.12 does not allow a flexible cord to stand in for a building's fixed wiring. A cord under a rug also hides wear and heat.

The spacing rule, NEC 210.52(A), counts any stretch of wall 2 feet wide or more in living rooms, bedrooms, dining rooms and similar rooms. Under 210.52(H), a hallway 10 feet or longer needs an outlet too. Spacing is only half of it, since every outlet on a circuit shares one breaker.

A new outlet goes on a circuit that still has spare capacity. When the old one is full, it goes on a new circuit with its own breaker. Both are licensed work in Chicago. The City's license page says any wiring its electrical code covers needs a City electrical contractor license, permit or not (chicago.gov, read October 17, 2026). Highlights Chicago holds ECC96456 and ECC94521.

How does a new outlet get into a plaster or brick wall in a Chicago building?

Usually from below, from above or along the surface, not through a long trench in the plaster. In a frame house the wire can often rise from the basement into a stud space; on brick it may run in surface conduit.

Many older Chicago interiors are plaster, either on wood lath over studs or laid straight onto the brick of outside and party walls. Plaster is hard and brittle, and a careless cut can crack it well past the opening.

The quick route is a long channel cut across the wall from the nearest outlet. It leaves a long patch to fill, sand and paint, and plaster on lath can loosen along the cut.

Chicago's code generally keeps new wiring in metal raceway rather than the plastic-sheathed cable common in the suburbs. Routes follow the paths named in the site checks above. Flexible metal conduit can often be fished through a closed stud space, so small openings at the box replace the long channel.

Where an outlet job is on a City permit, Highlights Chicago is the contractor of record. The route chosen for each wall is then part of that permitted work, not a shortcut around it.

Do new and replaced outlets in a Chicago home have to be tamper-resistant?

In most of a home, yes. Section 406.12 of the 2017 NEC calls for listed tamper-resistant receptacles on 15- and 20-amp outlets in dwellings, garages included.

A tamper-resistant receptacle has spring shutters behind its slots. The shutters open only when two prongs press in together, so a key or hairpin pushed into one slot meets plastic, not metal.

The common fix in older homes is a plastic plug cap, which comes out easily and goes missing. Standard receptacles without shutters also still sell in bulk packs, and fit the same box.

Section 406.4(D)(5) applies the rule at replacement: when an outlet in a covered area is swapped, the new one must be tamper-resistant. Section 406.12 exempts outlets more than 5½ feet above the floor and outlets that are part of a light fixture or appliance. It also exempts an outlet in a dedicated space for an appliance not easily moved, such as a refrigerator. A two-prong outlet replaced with another two-prong outlet, as 406.4(D)(2)(a) allows, is exempt as well.

That makes the shuttered device the default for new outlets and for grounded replacements in bedrooms, living rooms and garages. Customers describe Highlights Chicago jobs passing City inspection.
