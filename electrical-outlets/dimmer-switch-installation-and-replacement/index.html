Dimming from a second location: three routes

A new control location does not always mean new wire.

Most Chicago wiring runs in metal conduit, so travelers between two boxes can often be pulled through pipe that is already there. Where the pipe is full or missing, a smart companion or a remote does the job.

  • Rewire as a true 3-way

    Travelers run between the two boxes, and a 3-way dimmer pairs with a 3-way switch at the other end.

  • A smart main and companion

    The smart dimmer carries the load, and a companion at the second box signals it over the existing traveler or wirelessly, depending on the maker.

  • A wireless remote

    A battery remote in a wall plate needs no box and no wire, where the dimmer system supports one.

Site assessment

What gets checked before a dimmer quote

Most of the price, and most flicker problems, come down to what sits behind the wall plate.

  1. 01

    A neutral in the box

    Most smart dimmers need a neutral wire. Many older Chicago switch boxes hold only the power feed and the wire to the light.

  2. 02

    Box depth and box fill

    NEC 314.16 limits how many wires and devices a box may hold. A dimmer is bulkier than the switch it replaces, and shallow boxes run out of room.

  3. 03

    What the lights are

    Dimmable LED lamps, integrated LED fixtures and LED tape on a driver each dim differently. The dimmer has to suit that load.

  4. 04

    Total load and minimum load

    The wattage on the circuit has to sit between the dimmer's minimum and its LED rating. Two or three LED lamps can fall below the minimum.

  5. 05

    Dimmers side by side

    Dimmers ganged in one box can lose capacity under the maker's instructions. That derating can change the model.

  6. 06

    Where the light is switched from

    A light controlled from two or three places needs 3-way or 4-way devices. The wiring between the boxes decides which devices fit.

  7. 07

    Grounding and box condition

    In older buildings the metal conduit often serves as the ground path. Loose connections, brittle insulation or a cracked box need fixing before a new device goes in.

  8. 08

    The wall around the box

    Plaster and lath crack when a box is enlarged. How the wall is opened, and who patches it, is worth agreeing up front.

If the box has no neutral

  1. 1

    Pull a neutral through the conduit

    Where the pipe has room, a neutral can often be pulled from the light or a nearby box without opening the wall.

  3. 2

    A no-neutral smart dimmer

    Some smart models run without a neutral, but they need enough connected load to work properly.

  5. 3

    A standard LED dimmer

    A non-smart dimmer rated for LED loads needs no neutral and usually fits a shallower box.

Which route suits a given box depends on what is inside it.

01Does every dimmer job need a smart dimmer or new wiring?

No. Many dimmer jobs are a straight swap of the switch for an LED-rated dimmer, with no new wire at all. A smart dimmer, a pulled neutral or new travelers come in only when the household wants app control or a second location, or when the box is too small. Highlights Chicago can price the simpler route when it suits the lights.

02Can any LED bulb go on a dimmer?

No. The lamp has to be marked dimmable, and an integrated LED fixture needs a dimmable driver. A non-dimmable LED on a dimmer can flicker, buzz or fail early. Mixing lamp brands on one dimmer can also make them dim unevenly, so one lamp model per circuit tends to behave best.

03Can a ceiling fan go on a dimmer?

Not on a light dimmer. A fan motor needs a control made for fan speed, and a light dimmer can make it hum or run hot. Where the fan has a light kit, a combined fan and light control handles both from one box. That works when the box has room and the wiring allows separate control.

04Is it normal for a dimmer to feel warm?

Warm, yes. A dimmer turns a little energy into heat as it works. That is why every model carries a wattage limit, which matters more as lights are added to it. Hot to the touch, discolored or smelling of plastic is not normal, and the device needs an electrician's attention.

05Can a dimmer control a wall outlet?

A standard light dimmer should not control a switched outlet. Anything could be plugged in, from a lamp to a vacuum or a space heater, and a light dimmer is not made for those loads. A switched outlet keeps a plain switch, and a lamp that should dim gets a plug-in lamp dimmer. A dimmer found on an outlet circuit is replaced with a plain switch or a control listed for that load.

06Who can install a dimmer in a Chicago home?

Electrical work that needs a City of Chicago permit is done by a licensed electrical contractor, who is named on the permit. Whatever the job, power to the circuit goes off at the breaker before any device is touched. Highlights Chicago holds two active City of Chicago electrical contractor licenses, ECC96456 and ECC94521.

07Is a buzzing or flickering dimmer an emergency?

Usually not. Flicker and buzz most often come from a lamp and dimmer mismatch. A burning smell, a scorched wall plate or a dimmer too hot to touch is different, and the breaker should be switched off. Highlights Chicago offers emergency electrical service 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

08Do you cover my part of Chicago?

We work citywide, with the largest share of our permit record in Wards 1, 32, 26, 47 and 27 on the north and northwest sides. We also hold current registration in Evanston and Palatine.

Why the age matters electrically

A building from 1906 was wired, if it was wired at all, for lighting. Everything since — the refrigerator, the window units, the home office, the induction range, the car — has been added to a service that was never designed for it. That is the single thread running through most Chicago electrical work.

Guide 04 / 04

How dimmers work with LED lights in Chicago homes

A dimmer controls how much power reaches the lights. With LED lamps, the match between the dimmer, the lamp and the wiring matters more than it did with incandescent bulbs.

How often is dimmer work filed as a City permit?

Rarely as its own item. The City's permit categories have no dimmer category, so dimmer work cannot be counted on its own.

The closest label is a form action for repairing or altering devices on existing circuits. It appeared on 2,625 Express Permit Program electrical-scope permits citywide in the 24 months to September 2026. That label covers switches, outlets and other devices alike.

Dimmers also travel inside bigger lighting jobs, such as new recessed lights or a kitchen remodel. Highlights Chicago's own record is counted by the same City categories, so its dimmer work does not show as a separate number either. A low count reflects how the work is filed, not how often it is done.

Why do so many older Chicago switch boxes have no neutral?

For decades, a switch only had to interrupt the hot wire. Electricians often brought power to the light first and ran a two-wire loop down to the switch, so the switch box held no neutral.

A plain switch or a standard dimmer does not need one. Most smart dimmers do, because their electronics and radio need a small, steady supply that the neutral provides.

Codes caught up in the 2011 NEC, which added section 404.2(C) requiring a neutral at most switch locations. Chicago's housing is far older: 58.6% of its single-family and 2 to 6 unit buildings were built before 1940 (Cook County Assessor, tax year 2025).

Chicago applies the rule through the Chicago Electrical Code, which is based on the NEC. Older boxes installed before it remain common, which is why the neutral question comes up so often here.

What changes when the dimmer is in a Chicago condo?

Many Chicago condos sit in converted two-flats, courtyard buildings and high-rises. The owner controls the unit's own lighting circuits, but walls shared with a hallway or a neighbor often fall under the association.

A dimmer swap inside a unit rarely touches anything shared. The question changes when a neutral or traveler has to be pulled, or a box enlarged, in a wall the association considers common.

Association rules can also set working hours and ask the contractor for proof of insurance before work starts. Highlights Chicago is licensed and insured, which is the paperwork building managers often ask to see.

What is the difference between leading-edge and trailing-edge dimmers?

Both work by cutting off part of each alternating-current wave. A leading-edge dimmer cuts the start of each half wave, the method built for incandescent lamps and magnetic transformers.

A trailing-edge dimmer cuts the end of each half wave instead. Many LED lamps and electronic drivers run quieter and smoother on it, which is why some LED buzz fades with a trailing-edge model.

A third method skips the wave altogether. Fixtures and drivers marked 0-10V dim over two separate low-voltage control wires, common in commercial LED lighting and some home fixtures, and they need a matching 0-10V control.

Halogen track and low-voltage fixtures, common before LED lamps arrived, were usually paired with leading-edge or magnetic low-voltage dimmers. When those fixtures are relamped to LED, the old dimmer is a frequent source of buzz.
