Site assessment

What gets checked before a charger is chosen

The panel, the distance and the building set most of the scope. These are the checks, and why each one matters.

  1. 01

    Room on the service

    A load calculation under NEC Article 220 adds the charger to everything the home already runs. Article 625 treats charging as a continuous load, so the breaker is sized at 125 percent of the charger's draw.

  2. 02

    Free spaces in the panel

    A 240-volt charger takes a two-pole breaker, which uses two spaces. A full panel can still take one through a subpanel.

  3. 03

    The route to the parking spot

    Every wall, floor and foot of distance the run crosses adds labor. Chicago's code generally calls for wiring in metal conduit, so the route is planned as conduit, not cable.

  4. 04

    Where the car parks

    A driveway, an attached garage, a detached garage and a building's parking deck each mean a different route and often a different power source.

  5. 05

    The grounding already there

    The City's Express Permit terms say deficiencies in the existing system, grounding and bonding included, are corrected as part of permitted electrical work (chicago.gov, read October 17, 2026). An old water-pipe ground can add a line to the job.

  6. 06

    The charger and the car

    The charger's maximum current, its connector (J1772, or the J3400 plug Tesla uses) and its safety listing set the circuit. The car's onboard charger caps how fast it can actually charge.

If the panel can't take a charger

  1. 1

    Turn the charger down

    A lower current setting, with a smaller breaker to match, can fit a tight service and still refill a typical commute overnight.

  3. 2

    Manage the load

    A load-management device, or a charger with that feature built in, slows or pauses charging while the house is busy.

  5. 3

    Add a subpanel

    When the service has spare amps but the panel has no spaces, a subpanel makes room for the charger circuit.

  7. 4

    Upgrade the service

    When the house really is out of room, the service gets bigger. A year of ComEd demand data, used under NEC 220.87, can sometimes show room that a calculation on paper does not.

Which route fits depends on the calculated load, the free spaces and how far the car is driven each day.

Repair, reset or a new circuit?

The cause decides the fix.

  • A worn receptacle or a loose connection is a repair.

  • A charger set too high is a settings change, made only after a fault is ruled out.

  • A circuit too small for the charger, or a panel with no room, means new wiring or one of the routes in the assessment checks.

Incentives

The federal 30C credit for home charging equipment cannot be claimed for property placed in service after June 30, 2026, so a charger installed now does not qualify (IRS Form 8911 instructions, irs.gov/instructions/i8911, read October 17, 2026). ComEd's residential charger rebate page (comed.com/about-us/clean-energy/electric-vehicle-charger-and-installation-rebate, read the same day) covers part of a Level 2 charger and its installation, including labor, conduit, cable and panel work. A ComEd-approved EV service provider submits the application, the account has to stay on a time-variant supply rate for at least three years, and rebates go first come, first served. ComEd's page says funds are limited and the program term ends December 31, 2026, or earlier if funds run out.

01Does adding a charger always mean a new electrical service?

No. Many Chicago houses can take a charger circuit on the panel they already have. The load calculation decides, and the routes under the assessment checks above cover a panel that comes up short. A bigger service is the answer only when the house really is out of room.

02Is a regular 120-volt outlet enough to charge an electric car?

For short daily drives, sometimes. The Department of Energy puts Level 1 at about 5 miles of range per hour and Level 2 at about 25. Eight hours on a 120-volt outlet adds about 40 miles for a mid-size car. The outlet should have little else on its circuit, and an extension cord should never stand in for an outlet near the car.

03Can a renter have a charger installed in Chicago?

Yes. The Department of Energy's summary of Illinois law (765 ILCS 1085/35) lets a renter install a Level 1 or Level 2 charger at the leased parking space. The renter must use a licensed installation contractor and provide a certificate of insurance. The landlord may require payment for the power and any wiring upgrades. Highlights Chicago states on its Yelp business page that it is licensed and insured.

04Can two electric cars charge from one garage?

Yes, though not from one circuit. Under the 2017 NEC, section 625.40 gives every charging outlet its own branch circuit with nothing else on it. Two chargers can still share the panel's capacity: linked units or a load-management system cap their combined draw. NEC 625.42 then lets the service and feeder be sized to that cap. Tesla's installer page lists power management among the Wall Connector settings.

05Will home charging raise the ComEd bill much?

It adds whatever energy the car takes, billed at the home's rate, so miles driven and the car's efficiency decide the increase. ComEd offers hourly pricing and a delivery time-of-day rate that can favor overnight charging. Many chargers also log what each session used.

06Should the charger be bought before the electrician comes?

It can wait. The load check sets the current the circuit can carry, and a charger bought first may be bigger than the panel allows. If one is already bought, its specifications get checked against the circuit. Reviews and Yelp photos show Highlights Chicago installing Tesla and ChargePoint units.

07Can a new charger reuse the old charger's circuit?

Often, if the old wire and breaker were sized for at least the new unit's current. A plug-in unit can use a sound 14-50 receptacle as it is. Heat marks at an old receptacle or breaker are a reason to replace them rather than reuse them, and a hardwired swap means new connections at the wall.

08Does a Chicago winter change a charger install?

It can. An outdoor charger needs a weather-rated enclosure, and a plug-in unit outside needs a cover that stays weatherproof with the plug in. Trench work goes on in winter too: Billy K.'s February 2026 review describes a Highlights Chicago crew digging a garage trench by hand in midwinter.

Why the age matters electrically

A building from 1906 was wired, if it was wired at all, for lighting. Everything since — the refrigerator, the window units, the home office, the induction range, the car — has been added to a service that was never designed for it. That is the single thread running through most Chicago electrical work.

Township by township

West Chicago is the oldest at 83% pre-1940 across 65,664 properties. Lake View follows at 71% across 23,046. Jefferson township — Portage Park, Jefferson Park, Irving Park — is younger at 60% pre-1940 but far larger at 112,923 properties, and its median year of 1927 puts most of it in the bungalow era. Rogers Park is the youngest of the north-side townships at a 1938 median.

What that means room by room

188,389 properties citywide are two-storey and 154,926 single-storey; 324,305 have full basements, which is where the panel usually lives and where access is usually straightforward. 107,336 properties have no garage at all — relevant the moment an EV charger enters the conversation. And 335,769 use a warm-air furnace against 98,849 on hot water or steam, which decides what kind of equipment circuits exist already.

Getting power to the parking spot in Chicago

Three questions that come up after the price and the permit, each with the Chicago detail that decides it.

How does a charger circuit get from the house to a detached garage?

Usually underground, in conduit buried across the yard, as one circuit for the charger or a feeder to a small garage subpanel. The route and the trench set most of the cost. Highlights Chicago reviews describe crews trenching by hand to garages and leaving the site clean.

On a typical Chicago lot the garage faces the alley at the back, with a yard or a concrete walk between it and the house. The panel usually sits in the basement at the other end.

Quotes for the run differ more than charger prices do. One strings an overhead wire from the house to the garage. Another breaks up the walk and leaves the concrete for someone else to replace.

The real choices are the route, the depth and what arrives at the garage. Chicago's code, Title 14E, builds on the 2017 NEC, whose section 300.5 sets burial depth by wiring method. A garage fed by a feeder needs its own grounding electrode under NEC 250.32.

Highlights Chicago's detached-garage reviews describe the route settled before anyone digs and one trench to the garage. Deciding then whether the garage also gets lights and a door opener keeps it to one dig.

YouTube S.'s January 2026 review says other companies had proposed an overhead wire or breaking up a concrete walkway. The Highlights Chicago crew trenched along the least invasive path to the garage instead, then cleaned up.

Where does the power come from in a condo, two-flat or high-rise garage?

From one of three places: the owner's own unit panel, the building's house panel with a submeter, or a new meter set by ComEd. That choice decides who pays for the charging and how far the circuit runs. Highlights Chicago reviews describe charger jobs in shared garages, including a run through concrete and a submeter on a building's supply.

The Cook County Assessor counts 133,662 buildings of 2 to 6 units in Chicago and dates 88.5% of them to before 1940. Each unit usually has its own meter and panel, while the garage is shared.

A fast quote takes power from the nearest panel. That can put one owner's car on the house meter, where every owner pays for it. The problem shows up later, at an association meeting.

Choosing the power source comes first. The City's Express Permit page asks for a condo association approval letter for work on condo property. It also asks for drawings with load calculations in two cases (chicago.gov, read October 17, 2026). One is charging equipment serving 4 or more spaces; the other is new circuits in a residential building of 3 or more stories above grade.

Highlights Chicago's shared-garage reviews follow that order: the quote and the paperwork first, the association's approval next, the install last.

Marcel's September 2025 review describes a Tesla charger tied to his building's supply with a submeter, and an insurance certificate sent the day he asked. Bob B.'s October 2025 review describes a new 208-volt line run two floors through a concrete garage.

What is different about installing a Tesla Wall Connector?

The wiring matches any hardwired Level 2 charger; the setup does not. Tesla's installer page, read October 17, 2026, lists three required steps: commissioning in the Tesla One app, a Wi-Fi link and registration to the owner. Highlights Chicago reviews describe Tesla charger installs, and registration needs the email on the owner's Tesla account.

Tesla cars use the J3400 connector, also called NACS. The Department of Energy notes that every Tesla comes with a J1772 adapter (afdc.energy.gov, read October 17, 2026). So a Tesla owner can pick another brand of charger.

A charger is often treated as an appliance: hang it, wire it, switch it on. On a Wall Connector that can leave the unit unregistered and its settings unchecked.

Commissioning happens in the app, not on a dial: the installer scans the unit's QR code and enters the breaker size there. The Wi-Fi link needs a 2.4 GHz network that reaches the garage, and access can be limited to listed cars.

Highlights Chicago reviews place Tesla installs in detached garages and building garages. Both can sit beyond the reach of the house's Wi-Fi, so the router's range is worth checking before the visit.
