Old cans: three routes

A working can rarely has to come out of the ceiling.

Many older housings can take an LED trim made for them. The label inside the can lists the trims it was tested with, and that list decides the route.

  • LED retrofit trim

    An LED module screws into the old socket or wires in, then clips into the housing. The opening stays as it is.

  • A new housing, same opening

    Where the old can is the wrong type for the space above it, or rusted, a remodel housing goes in through the same hole.

  • A canless unit in the old opening

    The can comes out and a canless LED takes its place. A wider old opening needs a trim that covers it, or patching.

Illustrative photos

Which route fits depends on the housing label, what sits above it and the size of the old opening. The City's permit pages, read October 19, 2026, do not say whether a retrofit trim alone counts as permit work.

Site assessment

What gets checked before a recessed lighting quote

These checks, made above and around the ceiling, decide which fixture fits and how much of the ceiling opens.

  1. 01

    What sits above the ceiling

    Another heated floor, an attic, a flat roof or a concrete slab each call for a different fixture. Under a roof or an attic, insulation and air leakage come into it.

  2. 02

    Which way the joists run

    Which way the joists run decides how the wire reaches each light, and it cannot be seen from below.

  3. 03

    How deep the joist bays are

    A full housing needs height above the ceiling. A shallow bay under a flat roof, or a duct in the way, points to a canless unit.

  4. 04

    Plaster, drywall or both

    Plaster on wood lath cracks when it is cut carelessly. Drywall laid over old plaster adds half an inch or more to the thickness a fixture's clips must grip.

  5. 05

    Insulation above the ceiling

    Whether insulation lies over the planned spots decides the fixture rating; the library tab covers the rules.

  6. 06

    The circuit that will feed them

    LED downlights draw little, but an old lighting circuit is often shared with outlets. Its load is added up before more lights go on it.

  7. 07

    The switch box

    Several lighting zones mean several controls in one box, and a dimmer needs more room behind the plate than a plain switch.

  8. 08

    Pipes, ducts and beams

    A vent or a drain in the bay can block a planned spot. Finding it before cutting avoids an opening in the wrong place.

If the ceiling cannot take a housing

  1. 1

    A canless LED

    It needs only a shallow space above the ceiling, so it fits under ducts and flat roofs where a housing will not.

  3. 2

    A soffit or lowered section

    A built-down box carries the lights where the ceiling itself cannot be opened, such as a concrete slab.

  5. 3

    A surface or track fixture

    Where no opening is possible, a slim surface-mount LED or a track run lights the room from below the ceiling.

Which route fits depends on the depth above the ceiling, what is in the way and what the building allows.

New trims, new fixtures or new wiring?

Most problems with existing recessed lights stop at the trim.

  • A listed LED trim settles much of the cycling.

  • A scorched housing or brittle wire at the fixture is where the job grows into new fixtures or new wiring.

Cans or track a previous owner left

Many Chicago homes change hands with recessed cans or track runs already up, and nothing on the trim says what is above it. The label inside the housing gives the make, its ratings and the maximum wattage. A missing label is worth a look before new trims go in.

01Does adding recessed lights mean opening up the whole ceiling?

No. Remodel housings and canless LEDs go in through openings the size of the fixture, and the wire is usually fished from one opening to the next. A few extra access holes can be needed where a run is long or the switch is far away. The library tab explains how the joists set the layout.

02Can a recessed light go over a shower or tub?

Yes, with the right rating. Under 2017 NEC 410.10(D), a light inside the outline of a tub or shower needs a damp rating up to 8 feet above the rim or threshold. Where shower spray reaches it, it needs a wet rating. Trims made for showers usually carry that rating, with a lens and a gasket.

03Will the existing dimmer work with new LED recessed lights?

Often not. A dimmer bought for incandescent bulbs can flicker, buzz or drop out with LED fixtures, and a few LEDs can fall below its minimum load. Each fixture maker publishes the dimmers its lights were tested with, and the dimmer is picked from that list. Highlights Chicago's dimmer switch installation page covers the wall control in more depth.

04Do new recessed lights need a separate circuit?

Usually not. A dozen LED downlights at around 10 watts each use a small part of a 15-amp lighting circuit. Before a dozen downlights join an old lighting circuit, the contractor on the permit has to confirm the circuit can carry the added lights. Where an old circuit also feeds outlets and is already busy, a new circuit from the panel can be the better route.

05Can recessed lights go into a concrete ceiling in a Chicago high-rise?

Rarely into the slab itself. A concrete ceiling can hold conduit, cables or other services, and the association usually controls it. The usual answer is a soffit or lowered section built below the slab, or slim surface-mount LED downlights fed from the existing ceiling box. Reviews describe Highlights Chicago jobs in downtown high-rise condos, where the building's rules shape the plan.

06What color temperature suits recessed lights in a home?

Warm white, 2700K or 3000K, comes closest to the old incandescent look and is a common choice for living spaces. 3500K to 4000K reads cooler and suits garages and work areas. A color rendering index of 90 or above shows skin, wood and food more naturally. Some LED trims switch between color temperatures, which helps in a room with several uses.

07Is a hot or burnt-smelling recessed light an emergency?

It can be. A trim that smells burnt, looks scorched or is too hot to touch should be switched off at its breaker and left off. Smoke or flame calls for 911. A light that flickers or cycles off is not urgent, but it is worth having checked. Highlights Chicago offers emergency electrical service 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

08Do you cover my part of Chicago?

We work citywide, with the largest share of our permit record in Wards 1, 32, 26, 47 and 27 on the north and northwest sides. We also hold current registration in Evanston and Palatine.

Why the age matters electrically

A building from 1906 was wired, if it was wired at all, for lighting. Everything since — the refrigerator, the window units, the home office, the induction range, the car — has been added to a service that was never designed for it. That is the single thread running through most Chicago electrical work.

Township by township

West Chicago is the oldest at 83% pre-1940 across 65,664 properties. Lake View follows at 71% across 23,046. Jefferson township — Portage Park, Jefferson Park, Irving Park — is younger at 60% pre-1940 but far larger at 112,923 properties, and its median year of 1927 puts most of it in the bungalow era. Rogers Park is the youngest of the north-side townships at a 1938 median.

What that means room by room

188,389 properties citywide are two-storey and 154,926 single-storey; 324,305 have full basements, which is where the panel usually lives and where access is usually straightforward. 107,336 properties have no garage at all — relevant the moment an EV charger enters the conversation. And 335,769 use a warm-air furnace against 98,849 on hot water or steam, which decides what kind of equipment circuits exist already.

How recessed lights fit Chicago ceilings

A recessed light depends on the space above the ceiling as much as on the fixture. In Chicago that space is often old plaster, a shallow roof bay or a joist layout set a century ago.

Why does a plaster ceiling change a recessed lighting job in an older Chicago home?

Plaster on wood lath cracks and sheds when it is cut like drywall, and its thickness varies from room to room. That changes the cutting, the fixture clips and the patching. A Highlights Chicago review below describes a ceiling patch fitted between two electrical visits.

More than half of Chicago's houses and small residential buildings, 58.6% by Cook County Assessor records for tax year 2025, went up before 1940. Many of those ceilings were finished in plaster over wood lath.

The quick approach treats plaster like drywall: a hole saw at speed, a housing pushed into place, and clips sized for half-inch board. The lath snags and splits, plaster breaks loose around the hole, and the trim later sits on a crumbling edge.

Plaster rewards a slower cut, with the lath held firm while it is cut and the fixture chosen for the real ceiling thickness. Canless LEDs and remodel housings usually list the range of ceiling thickness the clips can grip.

Japheth L. described Highlights Chicago's electrician, Jimmy, taking the time to sort out the unusual wiring of an older house while replacing several light fixtures (August 2023). Where old wire at a ceiling box has to go, Highlights Chicago's electrical wiring and repair service covers the rewire.

Guido L. described a job done in two visits. The connections went in first, a painter patched the ceiling, and then the crew came back to install the lights (September 2023).

What changes when recessed lights go into a top-floor ceiling under a Chicago roof?

A top-floor ceiling is the line between heated rooms and a cold attic or roof, so each opening can leak warm air and meet insulation. Under the 2021 model energy code behind Chicago's own, those lights must be IC-rated, airtight and sealed. Highlights Chicago's home energy audit can show where else that ceiling loses heat.

Many Chicago two-flats and three-flats have flat roofs over shallow joist bays. Bungalows and frame houses more often have an attic above, sometimes with loose insulation blown across it.

Older top-floor jobs often used non-IC cans with the insulation pulled back, or with insulation laid straight over the cans. The first lets heat escape around every light; the second is the overheating the symptom table describes. 2017 NEC 410.116 keeps insulation at least 3 inches from a non-IC fixture and never over it.

Section R402.4.5 of the 2021 International Energy Conservation Code covers recessed lights in the building envelope. Each must be IC-rated, labeled for air leakage of 2.0 cfm or less, and sealed to the ceiling with a gasket or caulk. Chicago's Energy Transformation Code, Title 14N, is based on that edition (chicago.gov, read October 17, 2026). In a shallow flat-roof bay, an IC-rated canless LED is often the fixture that fits.

The City's Express Permit terms say light fixtures installed under an electrical permit must meet the Chicago Energy Transformation Code (chicago.gov, read October 17, 2026). Customers describe Highlights Chicago jobs passing City inspection, which is where that permitted work is checked.

That ceiling is common in Chicago's flats. Of the city's 2 to 6 unit buildings, 88.5% went up before 1940 (Cook County Assessor), decades before airtight fixtures were sold.

How do the joists and the ceiling height decide where recessed lights go?

A recessed layout starts with the joists and the ceiling height, not a grid on paper. Lights spaced at about half the ceiling height, in line with the joist bays, light a room evenly and need fewer extra openings. One Highlights Chicago review describes the crew helping plan where each light should sit.

Chicago ceilings hang from joists, often 16 inches apart, running between the bearing walls. Which way the joists run cannot be seen from below. An older room can also hide a beam or a doubled joist where a wall once stood.

The common plan draws an even grid and starts cutting. Lights then land on joists and shift a few inches, and rows drift out of line. A run across the joists needs extra openings to drill each one.

Finding the joists first changes the plan. A common starting point spaces the lights about half the ceiling height apart, so about 4 feet on an 8-foot ceiling. The lights nearest the walls sit about half that distance in. Rows that follow the joist bays share one wire run. NEC 300.4 sets how close to a joist's edge a drilled hole may sit.
